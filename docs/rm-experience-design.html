Agentic RM Workbench · design before code · v0.3

The RM Spine

Seven iterations gave the relationship manager fourteen good rooms. This design gives her one day that runs through them: a morning that is already read, a client that is already understood, a conversation where the numbers arrive as she speaks, promises that keep themselves, ideas that find their own clients, and a manager who sees the whole team's risk and conduct at a glance. It ends with the foundation for the day when the same brain speaks to clients directly.

01The thesis

Today the workbench is a chief of staff: it has read everything, it knows what the day is about, it prepares the paperwork, and it never acts in her name. Tomorrow the same brain, with the same memory and the same guardrails, will speak to clients through voice and chat. Everything we build now must be the same system in both states.

The test of every feature: does it remove a moment where the RM reconstructs a client in her head, and would it still be correct if an agent were doing it for her?

What changes from the first two drafts of this document is scope. The client context, the journey, the call plan and the conversational drawer stay. Around them we add what a professional RM reaches for a dozen times a day and has nowhere to put: a companion during the call, a ledger of promises after it, a desk that maps an idea to the clients it fits, and a page for the person who runs the team. And we add one thing that is not for the RM at all: a communication gateway and an autonomy ladder, so that when agents speak to clients they do it through the same checks, memory and audit that the RM does today.

03Principles

Written so they can be used to say no.

Start from the morning, not the schema

Every screen earns its place by answering a question the RM has at a specific hour. If we cannot name the hour, we do not build the screen.

Conclusion first, proof on demand

L1 says what, L2 says why, L3 shows the rows. The spine applies this to sentences and to the manager's numbers, not just to cards.

The system chooses, the RM confirms

No empty states asking her to pick. The call sheet orders, the journey opens on the top client, the drawer proposes. She overrides; she never starts from nothing.

One client at a time

Once a client is in context, everything is about that client until she leaves. Switching is deliberate, never a side effect.

Same brain, every channel

What the companion tells the RM, what the drawer tells the RM, and what an agent will one day tell a client come from the same tools, memory, guardrails and audit. No second system for clients.

Autonomy is earned, measured, and revocable

Agents move up the ladder only when shadow-mode agreement says they are ready, one playbook at a time, and a manager can send them back down with one switch.

Language is an input

Typing or saying a sentence is the fastest way to express intent. Language becomes an insight or a proposed task with the same evidence contract as every card.

Nothing acts, everything logs

The workbench prepares and proposes. Approval is a human click; the audit trail records who, what and why.

Honest about data

Where the dataset cannot support a number, the screen says so and shows the shape of the answer, never a fabricated figure.

Part one · The journey

04Six moments of the day

The journey is not a set of screens. It is six moments, each with one thing it must make feel effortless. Priscilla Ong covers twenty clients across two booking centres; this is her Tuesday.

08:30Wake

Today

She opens the app. No dashboard. A note from her chief of staff: four sentences on the book, what moved since the last snapshot, and the call sheet with the order, the hour and the reason for each conversation. Below, the three lanes for when she wants the detail, and "decided yesterday" so the day has a beginning.

"Good morning. Lau's collateral will breach at the next snapshot; call him before ten Hong Kong time. Zhang and Voss-Brenner can wait until Wednesday. One review to schedule."
Draws on: book cockpit, call plan (section 7), signals, audit log. Brief via Claude when live, template otherwise.
08:45Prepare

The client journey

She taps Lau. One page in five chapters, read top to bottom: where he stands, what happened, what could happen, what to do, what she decided. Every number links to its rows. The talking points, the sensitivities from his notes, and the moments ahead sit at the top of chapter 4. Eight minutes, one screen, and she knows him again.

Prose that knows its sources: hover any figure and the rows that produced it appear. Explainability is the text, not a panel.
Draws on: every existing client endpoint, notes, moments timeline, call plan. Section 6.
09:00Converse

The companion

She calls Lau with the companion open beside the journey. As he talks she types three-word cues, or dictates them: "asking about selling perpetual". The companion answers in a second with the number, the rule, the suitability status and what she may and may not say to a Hong Kong resident. When he mentions the November project, the cash-need card is already there. When the call ends, the note is drafted, the two promises she made are listed, and the suitability record is pre-filled.

Say it, and the note writes itself. Ask it, and the number is there before the client finishes the sentence.
Draws on: impact, exposure, actions, rubric, notes memory, cross-border policy, peers. Section 8.
09:40Follow through

The promise ledger

Two promises from the call appear as tasks with drafts attached: the confirmation email in Traditional Chinese, and the yield ideas he asked for, already screened for suitability. She edits one line, sends, and the log writes itself. The collateral action goes to her checker with the evidence attached. Nothing is on a sticky note.

A promise made in a call is a task before she puts the phone down, with the first draft done.
Draws on: outreach drafter, workflow, audit, promise extraction. Section 9.
11:00Grow

The idea desk

The house view says short-duration credit. She asks: who should hear this? The desk answers with six clients, each with a one-line why, the suitability gate, and the cross-border check, and offers six personalised drafts in six languages. Beneath, the opportunities lane: Lau's cash need is a lending conversation; Zhang's concentration is a discretionary-mandate conversation; Hartono's father is a succession conversation.

Ideas find their own clients. One sentence becomes a campaign with the compliance already done.
Draws on: trade ideas engine inverted, customer vectors, suitability, policy. Section 10.
17:30Reflect

My week

Before she leaves: what she decided, what slipped and why, which promises are due tomorrow, which clients are drifting past their cadence, and what the machine did on her behalf today. Tomorrow's call sheet is already drafted.

The day has a shape and an ending. Nothing is carried in her head overnight.
Draws on: audit log, promise ledger, call plan, deferrals.

  • The client. Source of truth is the URL; a small store mirrors it so the rail, the drawer, the companion and the strip agree. Leaving the client screens keeps the last client as current.
  • The clock. Already global; shown in brass only when it is not today, so a replay is never mistaken for the present.

Chapters, not tabs

Tabs hide. A scroll reveals the order of reasoning; the chapter rail shows where she is.

Prose with numbers, then charts

Chapter openers are sentences composed from computed facts, each carrying its source count. Claude rewrites them when live and is marked as such; it never adds a fact.

Decisions inline

Approve, reject, triage and send live in chapters 4 and 5. The Workflow room remains for review detail and maker-checker.

Reading budgets

Chapter 1 in ninety seconds; the page in eight minutes. If a chapter grows past a screen, we cut.

Whom: the priority score

Term What it captures How it is computed Source
1 · Harm What is at stake if nothing is said. The existing urgency: Σ over open items of lane rank (Now 3, week 2, month 1) × severity (high 3, medium 2, low 1) + 1 per new or escalated item. Horizon engine
2 · Clock How soon each item bites. Each item multiplied by τ = 1 + max(0, 1 − days-to-deadline / 30). Deadlines from KYC due, cash-need start less a lead time, moments from notes; a facility with falling headroom gets a projected breach date (headroom ÷ fall per snapshot × interval); a severe signal gets a three-business-day freshness window. Alerts, LTV trend, moments, policy
3 · Convergence One conversation, several outcomes. +2 per distinct theme beyond the first among Now and week items. Horizon themes
4 · Relationship debt Overdue for contact regardless of alerts. Days since last note versus cadence for the wealth band (policy: UHNW 30, HNW 60). +1 per 30 days overdue, capped at +4. Open promises past due add +1 each, capped at +3. Notes, promise ledger, policy
5 · Recency damper Do not call twice about the same thing. An item whose theme appears in a note from the last seven days is halved, unless it escalated since. Notes, momentum

Priority = Σitems harm × clock × damper + convergence + relationship debt. Ties break by money at stake, then AUM. Every term is a row in L2 with its inputs, in the style of the rubric's rule contributions.

When: deadline and slot

Due by

Earliest hard date among the client's items and moments; if none, the lane decides: Now today, week within five business days, month within twenty. Shown as a day, not a score.

Slot

Business hours in the client's local time from a country-to-timezone table in the reference data, intersected with the RM's hours. Weekends skipped; holidays when a calendar is supplied.

Channel and language

The client's most frequent channel in the notes, forced to a call for margin calls and unanswered messages, to a meeting for reviews. Language from the reporting language.

Call or schedule

Annual reviews and KYC refreshes are meetings to schedule, shown on their own line so they do not compete with today's collateral call.

Packing the day

Sort by due-by then priority; pack into the RM's capacity from the policy file (six conversations of thirty minutes by default, longer for meetings). Overflow moves to the next business day with the reason visible. Deferrals carry a reason, are logged, and hold the client out until the chosen day; an escalation brings the client back marked "returned early". Done comes from the workflow's send or log step; the RM never ticks a box the system already knows about.

Why deterministic

A ranking the RM must argue with cannot be a learned score. Five terms with visible inputs can be argued with. Deferral logs are kept from day one so the weights can be calibrated later, per RM, if you choose. Weights, cadences, lead times, capacity, hours and the timezone table live in a reference file beside the signal rules: another bank, another book, change the file, not the code.

How it works

Cues, not transcripts

The RM types or dictates short cues. Dictation uses the browser's speech recognition, which needs no server and no recording of the client. Full call transcription is a later step that needs telephony, consent and recording policy; the design accepts it as another cue source without change.

Answers from tools only

Each cue goes to the same intent router as the drawer with the client fixed. Numbers come from the exposure, impact, actions and rubric services; words from the notes; nothing else. The tool trace is one click away. Without an API key, a command grammar covers "sell X", "impact of Y", "cash need", "peers".

Guardrails are data

A cross-border policy file keyed by residence and booking centre says what may be discussed, offered or sent, with the rule text. Suitability comes from the existing gate. The companion shows the guardrails before the call and flags a cue that crosses one.

Memory that quotes

Sensitivities and preferences are quoted from notes with their date, never inferred. "He said in June he wants to keep the perpetuals for income" is a citation.

The record writes itself

The note drafts from the cues and the answers; promises are extracted as candidate tasks the RM confirms; the suitability record pre-fills from the discussed idea. Ending the call produces one confirm screen and then the audit events.

Nothing is sent from the companion

It drafts and proposes. Sending, approving and booking happen in the follow-through step with the usual clicks.

09Follow through: the promise ledger

Advice dies in the gap between the call and the follow-up. The ledger closes it.

What a promise is

A commitment with a party, a thing, and a due date. RM promises ("send yield ideas"), client promises ("top up collateral by Friday"), and decision debt: an allocation agreed and not executed, found in the notes.

Where they come from

Extracted from historical notes by Claude with the sentence quoted, from the companion's cues at call end, and from the RM directly. Every promise carries its source sentence.

What the ledger does

Attaches a first draft to every RM promise (outreach, idea list, summary pack), tracks client promises against the data (a collateral top-up shows in the facility snapshot), raises decision debt in the journey and the call plan, and marks done from the send or log event.

The "Since we last spoke" pack

One click produces a client-facing summary in the reporting language: what changed and why in three sentences, the attribution chart, the positions discussed, the actions agreed, and the standard caveats. It is the most common promise an RM makes and the most tedious to fulfil. It is generated from the journey's chapters 2 and 4, so it never disagrees with what the RM saw.

Reverse search

Idea rules already exist as data. Running them across the book with the suitability gate and cross-border check gives the client list; the customer vector gives "clients like the ones who took this idea before".

Campaign, not broadcast

One draft per client, in their language, from their facts, each reviewed and sent individually. Uptake is tracked: contacted, discussed, acted. The manager sees it in section 12.

Opportunities are rules with reasons

A lending opportunity is a cash need against liquid coverage; a mandate opportunity is repeated breaches; succession and next generation come from notes. Each shows its rule or its quoted sentence. No scoring theatre.

Revenue, honestly

Management-fee transactions give a fee proxy per client. Real revenue, RoA and pipeline value need a feed; the desk shows the shape with a "needs a feed" mark rather than a made-up number.

11Talking to the workbench

Language is the third input, alongside click and clock. The drawer, the companion and one day the client-facing agent all run on the same router. Four intents.

Grounding and guardrails

  • Tools are the only source of facts; the event log is the only world knowledge; cited figures are validated against tool results or the answer carries a warning.
  • Untrusted text (notes, event descriptions) is wrapped in data delimiters. This is the prompt-injection control the security review asked for, applied here first.
  • Client names are pseudonymised on the way to the model and restored on the way back.
  • Trading instructions are answered with the proposal for the desk and the reminder that nothing executes here.
  • Rate-limited and concurrency-capped through the existing gateway; every exchange traced.

Each panel is a question with an owner and a consequence: risk leads to a call, conduct to a conversation with an RM, coverage to a reassignment, the queue to a signature, capacity to help. Part three · The future

13Toward agents that speak to clients

Not now. But the target state shapes what we build now: one brain, one memory, one set of guardrails, one audit, and a ladder of autonomy that is climbed by evidence and can be descended by a switch.

L0Drafts onlyEverything the machine writes is a draft the RM edits and sends. Where we are.
L1Approve to sendMachine proposes finished messages and tasks; RM confirms each. Iteration 8 to 10.
L2Informational, notifiedAgent sends low-risk playbook messages (KYC reminder, meeting scheduling, statement explanation, "since we last spoke") itself; RM is notified and can recall within a window.
L3Converses within a playbookChat agent answers client questions from the same tools, in the client's language; escalates to the RM on advice, complaint, distress, or anything outside the playbook. RM sees the transcript live.
L4VoiceSame as L3 by voice, with disclosure, consent and recording. Warm handoff to the RM with a context summary.

What we build now so the ladder is real

Communication gateway

Every message to a client, whether the RM wrote it or the machine did, passes one gate: suitability, cross-border, language, disclaimers, tone, pseudonymisation, audit. Today the gate sits behind the send button. Tomorrow an agent calls the same gate.

Playbooks as data

A playbook is a structured conversation with a goal, allowed topics, required disclosures, escalation triggers and a done condition, stored as JSON. The RM runs playbooks today through the companion; an agent runs the same file later. First four: KYC refresh, cash-need check-in, market update, meeting scheduling.

Shadow mode

For each playbook, the agent drafts what it would have said at every step of a real RM interaction. The RM grades the draft in one tap. Agreement per playbook is the number that earns L2. The manager sees it. Nothing reaches the client.

One memory

Notes, promises, sensitivities, decisions and transcripts are one client memory with source and date on every entry, readable by RM and agent alike. There is no second memory for agents.

Escalation and handoff

Triggers are data: advice requests, complaints, distress language, trade instructions, anything outside the playbook. Handoff produces a summary card the RM reads in ten seconds.

Consent, disclosure, kill switch

Client opt-in per channel, disclosure that the counterpart is an AI, recording rules per jurisdiction in the policy file. One switch on the manager's page pauses every agent and drops them to L1.

15What we remove

Removed or demoted
  • The module list as primary navigation; it becomes a collapsed "Rooms" group.
  • The client picker in every page header; the strip and ⌘K replace it.
  • Default-client redirects; Today decides the first client.
  • Duplicate headers between Client 360 and the journey's chapter 1.
  • Separate screens for note-taking, suitability record and follow-up; the companion produces all three.
  • The habit of opening six screens to prepare one call.
Kept, deliberately
  • All fourteen rooms and their depth; the journey links to them.
  • The replay clock; the journey, call sheet and team page all rewrite themselves at any date. Backtesting the call plan against history is one of the demo's strongest moments.
  • The L1 / L2 / L3 contract on every card, sentence and manager number.
  • Human approval on every action, maker-checker on money-moving ones, the append-only log.
  • Dataset agnosticism: nothing in the spine names a client, a date or an event; policy lives in reference files.

18Decisions for you

Each with my recommendation. None blocks iteration 8a.

  2. Which tasks may the drawer and companion propose?Everything that exists as an endpoint, or a smaller set.Recommended: all except send. Sending stays a click on the reviewed draft.
  3. Prose openers: model or template?Recommended: template first; Claude rewrites the same facts when live, marked, never adding a fact.
  4. Cue input: typed, dictated, or both?Browser speech recognition costs nothing and records nothing server-side.Recommended: both, with dictation as a toggle. Full transcription waits for telephony and consent.

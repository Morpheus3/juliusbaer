Private Banking Discovery Questions

The Global Markets Agentic AI Opportunity Map is organised by desk and product: FX and Treasury, Equities, Funds, Bonds and Structured Products, each with front, middle and back office and an agentic rating per function. Private Banking has the same instruments but a different centre of gravity. The client is the unit of work, every transaction is a suitability question, and the person who has to act is a relationship manager with a book. These are the questions to ask a bank so that the RM workbench we have built can be fed from its systems, asset class by asset class.

01What the map says, and what changes

The Global Markets map answers "which desk functions can an agent run". Private Banking needs to answer "what does this client's household look like right now, what changed, who may see it, and what should the RM do next". Same instruments, different axis.

So the questions below are not about automating operations. They are about the four things the RM workbench needs from a bank's data estate: a client-centred golden record, positions and transactions per asset class with known timing, the events that say something changed, and the entitlement that says which RM may see it.

Keep from the map

The asset-class decomposition, the product and transaction lists per class, the corporate-action catalogue, the reference-data layer (party, account, instrument master, pricing, collateral, limits), the risk and compliance layer, and the front, middle, back office split as a way to find the system of record for each step.

Change for Private Banking

Channel is the RM and the client portal, not the dealing desk. Suitability, appropriateness and cross-border rules sit in front of every order. Advisory versus discretionary versus execution-only decides who initiates. Lombard lending, mandates, cash needs and the RM's notes become first-class data. Retail regulation adds cooling-off, product risk ratings, vulnerable-client flags and complaints.

Missing from the map

Nothing about the client: no household view, no relationship history, no RM-to-client assignment, no source-system timing. No event model: the map lists functions, not the changes that flow between them. No isolation: who may see which client. The workbench is built on exactly those three things, so the questions concentrate there.

03Functional questions by asset class

Asked of the business owners of each asset class. Each block covers the products in scope, the lifecycle events that matter to a client, the controls in front of a transaction, and what the RM is expected to do about them.

Cash, deposits and FX

  1. Which cash products does a client hold: current accounts, term deposits, structured deposits, dual-currency investments, FX accumulators or decumulators? Which are booked as cash and which as structured products in the bank's own classification?Decides how the Cash and Equivalents and Structured Products classes are populated and how liquidity tiers are assigned.
  2. How is "available liquidity" defined for a client: cleared balances, uncleared, pledged, under notice, held for settlement? Which of these may the RM quote as sellable or drawable?Feeds the daily-liquid coverage of cash needs and the liquidity shortfall alert.
  3. Which FX transactions are client-initiated and which are funding conversions the bank performs for settlement? Are conversion rates and spreads visible per transaction?Separates the FX effect in attribution from client decisions.
  4. For term deposits and structured deposits: where are maturity, rollover instruction, early-withdrawal penalty and knock-in or knock-out levels recorded, and when does the client get told?Maturities are cash events the call plan should see before they happen.
  5. What happens at an FX accumulator fixing against the client: who is notified, when, and is the settlement visible as a transaction or only as a position change?The dataset showed accumulator settlements running against a client for months; the RM needs the event, not the aftermath.

Equities, ETFs and IPO

  1. Which service model applies to each portfolio: discretionary, advisory, execution-only, or custody-only? Who initiates orders in each, and which are in scope for the RM's suitability duty?The workbench measures mandate bands only on managed portfolios and shows custody separately.
  2. What single-position and issuer concentration limits apply, at portfolio and household level, and do they look through ETFs, funds and structured notes to the underlying issuer?Look-through concentration is the workbench's most visible alert; the bank's limit definition must match.
  3. How are IPO subscriptions, IPO financing and allotment represented before listing: as a pending order, a receivable, a loan, or nothing until settlement?Pre-listing exposure otherwise disappears from the household view.
  4. For regular savings plans: where is the standing instruction, the next execution date and the funding account recorded? Are missed executions surfaced anywhere?Both a cash-flow item and a decision-debt signal.
  5. Which corporate actions reach the RM as something to discuss (rights, takeovers, scrip dividends, voluntary conversions) versus processed silently (cash dividends, splits)? Who decides the client's election and by when?Voluntary events with deadlines belong in the call plan's clock term.
  6. Is realised and unrealised profit and loss computed by the custody system, the performance system, or both? Which cost basis method, and in which currency?The journey's "what happened" chapter must agree with the client statement.

Funds and alternatives

  1. Which fund types are held: daily-dealing unit trusts, money-market funds, funds with gates or lock-ups, private equity and private credit with capital calls, hedge funds with monthly or quarterly liquidity?Each maps to a liquidity tier the coverage ratio depends on.
  2. For committed capital: where are total commitment, called to date, uncalled and expected call window recorded, and how does a capital call notice arrive?Uncalled commitments are dated cash needs the RM must fund.
  3. How stale can a fund valuation be before it is flagged, and is the valuation date carried with the position? Who owns the NAV feed for private funds?Stale-valuation alerts and the confidence band on impact depend on this.
  4. Fund switches: are same-house and cross-house switches one transaction or a redemption plus subscription with cash in between? How long is the client out of the market?Affects attribution flows and the RM's explanation of performance gaps.
  5. Which fund corporate actions (mergers, liquidations, closures, series roll-ups) require a client decision, and how is the client's election captured?Dated decisions go to the call plan.

Bonds and structured products

  1. For structured notes and equity-linked notes: is the payoff decomposed anywhere (underlyings, barriers, coupons, autocall dates), or is the note a single line with a price?The workbench's look-through uses a reference file today; the bank may have a structuring system that holds the legs.
  2. Which bond lifecycle events reach the client and the RM: coupon, partial or full call, maturity, rating change, default or restructuring? Where is the call schedule held?Calls and maturities are cash events; downgrades are signals.
  3. Are bonds and notes pledged as collateral marked as such on the position, with the lending value and advance rate per line?Lending value per holding drives the LTV recomputation under stress.
  4. Which product risk rating scheme applies (bank internal, regulatory), and how does it map to the client's risk profile for suitability? Where is the appropriateness assessment for complex products recorded?The suitability gate on actions and ideas needs the bank's own rule set, not ours.
  5. For perpetuals and subordinated debt sold for income: is the income expectation (coupon reset, call probability) captured anywhere the RM can quote?Income yield is a KPI on the client's front door.

Lending, collateral and mandates

  1. Which facilities exist against a household: Lombard lines, property-backed loans, overdrafts, IPO financing? Is each linked to the specific collateral portfolio?The facility-to-portfolio link is what makes LTV explainable line by line.
  2. How often is LTV recomputed (intraday, end of day), what are the trigger, top-up and close-out levels, and how much notice does the client get?Headroom trend and the projected breach date in the call plan need the recomputation cadence.
  3. Who may waive or extend a margin call, and is the waiver recorded as structured data or in a note?The board reads "waived" from notes today; a structured flag is better.
  4. For discretionary and advisory mandates: where are the strategic bands, target weights, single-position limits and exclusion lists held, and how are client-directed deviations recorded?Mandate bands and client-directed breaches are core to the risk matrix and the conduct panel.
  5. What is the bank's stated risk-profile scale, investment-horizon question set and review frequency, and where are stated versus observed differences expected to be reconciled?The rubric compares stated profile with observed behaviour; the bank's scale must be mapped, not assumed.

Cross-asset controls the RM lives under

  1. Which cross-border rules apply by client residence and booking centre: what may be discussed, offered, sent, and what needs documented reverse enquiry?The companion's guardrails and the communication gateway use a reference file today; the bank's compliance owns the real one.
  2. What must be recorded for each advised transaction: suitability confirmation, product disclosure acknowledgement, call note, recorded line? Where does each live?The workflow's suitability record and the gate's disclosures must match the bank's evidence requirements.
  3. Which actions need a second pair of eyes (collateral changes, rebalancing, liquidity raising) and who qualifies as checker?Maker-checker levels in the workflow are configurable; the bank's matrix decides them.
  4. How are complaints, vulnerable-client flags and dormant or deceased-client statuses represented, and must they stop outreach?Escalation triggers and the gateway need structured flags rather than free text.

Questions that apply to every cell

  1. Which system is the system of record for this cell, and which systems hold copies? When they disagree, which wins?The workbench needs one golden source per domain to keep the L3 evidence honest.
  2. What is the identifier and how does it join to the client: client id, household id, portfolio or account id, instrument id (ISIN, internal, CUSIP)? Is the mapping stable across systems and over time?Every derived table carries the client id; joins are the whole game.
  3. When does the cell update: real-time on the transaction, intraday batch at fixed times, end of day, T+1 morning? In which time zone, and what is the cut-off for "today's" figures?Decides what "clock" means and whether the workbench can show today or only yesterday.
  4. Can a published value be restated later (late prices, corrected trades, NAV restatements, back-dated notes)? How is the restatement signalled?The attribution and the audit trail must not silently change.
  5. How much history is available online and how much in archive, per cell? Are point-in-time snapshots kept or only current state?The replay clock, momentum against the previous snapshot and stress windows need history with dates.
  6. Who owns data quality for the cell, which checks already run, and where are failures visible?The workbench's quality register should extend the bank's checks, not duplicate them.
  7. Which fields are personal data, which are restricted, and what may leave the bank's boundary to a model provider? Is pseudonymisation acceptable?Governs the Claude gateway, traces and the plugin design.
  8. Is the client's reporting currency conversion done at source or expected downstream, and with which rate and time?Household totals and FX effects are only comparable with one convention.

Client and relationship domain, specifically

  1. Where does the RM-to-client assignment live (CRM, core banking, HR), how are secondary RMs, checkers, team heads and holiday cover represented, and is assignment history kept with dates?Row-level security is built on dated assignments; without history, "who saw what in March" cannot be answered.
  2. Where are RM notes, call logs and meeting minutes captured, in which languages, and are they structured (channel, date, attendees) or free text? Is there a recorded-line system, and is transcription permitted?Notes feed the call plan, promises, sensitivities and the companion.
  3. Where do planned client cash needs, life events and objectives live: CRM fields, financial-planning tool, or only in notes?Dated needs drive the liquidity coverage and the call plan's deadlines.
  4. Where does a client message wait for a reply (secure mail, portal, messaging app), and is its timestamp available?The unanswered-contact alert is a Now item; it needs a source.
  5. Is the household a first-class entity (linked accounts, joint holders, trusts, beneficial owners), or does the bank report per account holder?The workbench aggregates by client; households need a defined key.

06Event enablement of source systems

The workbench ingests messages with one envelope and typed payloads, from Kafka or from files, and applies them idempotently. These questions establish whether each source can emit events, and if not, how change can be detected.

Per source system

  1. Does the system publish business events today (a message bus, webhooks, an outbox table), expose change data capture on its database, or only produce files? For each: which events, in which format, with what schema governance?Decides whether the Kafka front door or the batch adapter is the first integration.
  2. For the events it publishes, is there a stable idempotency key (source plus sequence or change id) and a business event time distinct from the publish time?The landing zone dedupes on the key; the change log orders on event time.
  3. Are events ordered per client or per account? Can a consumer partition by client id so one client's changes arrive in order?The consumer partitions by client to keep a household consistent.
  4. How are late, out-of-order and corrected events represented: a new event with a supersedes reference, a delete-and-replace, or a full snapshot?Restatements must be applied, not merely appended.
  5. Can the topic be replayed from an offset or a date, and how long is retention? Is there a bootstrap path for a new consumer, such as a full snapshot topic or an initial file drop?A new RM book or a rebuilt vector store needs a cold start.
  6. Which events carry personal data, and is field-level encryption or tokenisation applied on the bus?Determines what may sit in the landing zone and what must be masked before any model call.

Operational questions for the event platform

  1. Who owns topic naming, the schema registry and the versioning policy, and how are breaking changes announced to consumers?The message contracts are versioned; the bank's policy decides how long old versions are served.
  2. What are the expected volumes and peaks per topic (end-of-day bursts, month-end, corporate-action seasons)?Sizes the consumer batch and the vector rebuild strategy.
  3. Is there a dead-letter convention, and who triages rejected messages: the producer team or the consumer team?The workbench keeps a dead-letter view; someone must own it.
  4. Are there environments with production-like events for testing (masked replay, synthetic feeds)?Needed to prove ingestion before go-live.

08The fifteen to ask first

If the first workshop has ninety minutes, these decide whether the workbench can be fed at all.

  1. Which system is the golden source for the client record, and which for RM-to-client assignment with history? (D-1, D-C1)
  2. Is the household a defined entity with a key, or are we aggregating account holders? (D-C5)
  3. When are custody positions and prices final each day, in which time zone, and can they be restated? (D-3, D-4)
  4. Which portfolios are discretionary, advisory, execution-only or custody, and where is that flag? (F-E1)
  5. Where are mandate bands, single-position limits and exclusions codified, and do concentration limits look through? (F-L4, F-E2)
  6. How often is LTV recomputed and where are trigger levels and waivers recorded? (F-L2, F-L3)
  7. Are structured notes decomposed into legs anywhere, and are lending values carried per holding? (F-B1, F-B3)
  8. Where do RM notes, calls and client messages live, and in which languages? (D-C2, D-C4)
  9. Where do planned cash needs, commitments and capital-call schedules live? (D-C3, F-F2)
  10. What is the bank's risk-profile scale, and may stated be compared with observed? (J-10, J-11)
  11. Which suitability, appropriateness and cross-border rules must sit in front of every idea and message, and in what form can compliance supply them? (J-13, J-16)
  12. Which source systems can emit events today, with idempotency keys and per-client ordering, and which only produce files? (E-1, E-2, E-3)
  13. Which identity provider issues RM identities, and which claims carry role and team? (J-30)
  14. What may leave the bank to a model provider, and is pseudonymisation acceptable? (D-7)
  15. Who owns dead letters and data quality once the workbench consumes the feeds? (E-9, D-6)

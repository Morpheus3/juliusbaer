Agentic RM Workbench · discovery for Private Banking and Retail Wealth

Private Banking Discovery Questions

The bank's problem is a clock. Data lands at nine; the relationship manager needs her day ready at six: the brief, the calls, the client stories, the ideas and the tasks. These are the questions to ask so the workbench we built can be fed early enough, from the right systems, with the right entitlements, and so the agents that write content and reach clients do so within the bank's rules. Every question says why we ask it.

01The problem: 9 a.m. data, 6 a.m. need

Today the RM starts working when the data arrives. The goal is the reverse: when she opens the workbench at six, the machine has already read everything, ranked the book, written each client's story, run the signals against every household, found which clients an idea fits, drafted the messages, and lined up the tasks. The RM's morning is spent deciding, not assembling.

Three hours have to come from somewhere. Either the sources can deliver earlier or incrementally, or the workbench works from the previous close plus overnight events and refreshes when the day's batch lands. Which of those is possible is a question about each source system, and most of section 7 exists to answer it.

Today09:00 batch landsPositions, prices and balances arrive together; the RM reads, reconciles and plans until mid-morning.
Target04:00 to 05:30 machine runsOvernight events applied, vectors and alerts rebuilt, briefs and drafts written, call sheet packed, tasks proposed.
Target06:00 RM opens TodayBrief, call sheet, client journeys, ideas and drafts are ready. Anything that changes after the day's batch is marked as refreshed.
RuleNothing invented to fill the gapWhere a source cannot deliver by six, the screen shows the last known value with its timestamp, never a guess.

02What the RM does

The workbench is organised around six moments of the RM's day: wake, prepare, converse, follow through, grow, reflect, with the team head's Monday above them. These questions confirm the bank's RM day matches, and what she does that we have not seen.

  1. Walk us through an RM's day from arrival to leaving: what she reads first, how she decides whom to call, what she prepares before a call, what she does during and after it, and what she hands to others.Why we askThe six moments we built are a hypothesis; the bank's real sequence decides what must be ready at six and in what order.
  2. How many clients does an RM cover, how many conversations a day is normal, and what share of the day goes to preparation, documentation and chasing versus talking to clients?Why we askThe call plan's capacity and the efficiency case both rest on these numbers; the workbench should remove the preparation and documentation share.
  3. What does the RM assemble by hand today before a client call, and from how many screens or systems?Why we askThat assembly is the client journey's five chapters; whatever she gathers manually is what the machine must pre-build.
  4. How does the RM decide whom to call first today, and whom she can leave until next week? Is it gut, a report, or a manager's list?Why we askThe call plan ranks by harm, deadline, bundling and relationship debt; the bank's actual heuristics should become its policy file, not be replaced by ours.
  5. What must the RM record after every interaction (call note, suitability confirmation, disclosures given, promises made), where, and how long does it take?Why we askThe companion drafts the note, extracts promises and pre-fills the suitability record; the bank's evidence requirements define what "complete" means.
  6. Which decisions need a second person (checker, team head, compliance), and how long do those approvals take today?Why we askMaker-checker is configurable in the workflow; approval latency is a task the machine can queue and chase before six.
  7. What does the RM do when a market event happens intraday: who tells her, how does she find which clients are affected, and what is she expected to do by when?Why we askSignals mapped to holdings and the impact engine automate exactly this; the expected response time decides whether intraday events must flow, not just the overnight batch.
  8. What does the team head look at on Monday morning, and what does she ask RMs for by email today?Why we askThe team page has five panels built on a guess of those questions; the real ones replace them.
  9. Which parts of the day would the RM most like to stop doing, and which would she never hand to a machine?Why we askSets the autonomy ladder honestly: drafts only, approve to send, or informational messages sent by an agent.

03Entitlements for the RM

One RM must not see another's clients. The workbench enforces this in the database with row-level security driven by dated RM-to-client assignments. These questions establish where that truth lives in the bank.

  1. Where is the RM-to-client assignment held (CRM, core banking, HR), and is history kept with valid-from and valid-to dates?Why we askEntitlement is computed from assignments valid today; the audit trail needs history to answer who could see a client in a given month.
  2. Besides the primary RM, who else legitimately sees a client: secondary RM, assistant, checker, team head, holiday cover, product specialist, compliance? Is each a role with a duration?Why we askOur assignment model has primary, secondary, checker and cover roles with dates; the bank's list decides which roles exist and whether cover is time-boxed.
  3. How is a team or desk defined, who heads it, and may a head see every client in the team or only aggregates?Why we askTeam scope in row-level security is the head's whole team; if the bank wants aggregates only, the team page must change.
  4. Which identity provider issues RM and manager identities, and which claims carry role, team and RM id?Why we askThe API verifies a token and maps its claims to scope; production needs the bank's issuer, audience and claim names.
  5. When a client is reassigned, how quickly must the old RM lose access and the new RM gain it, and who approves the reassignment?Why we askAn assignment message updates access the moment it is applied; the bank's expectation decides whether that must be an event or can wait for a nightly file.
  6. May any book-wide computation happen (percentiles, peer groups, which clients fit an idea) as long as an RM only ever sees her own clients in the result?Why we askThe analytics service computes peers and percentiles across the bank under a service role; the idea desk searches the whole book then filters to the caller's scope.
  7. What may an RM see about a client after offboarding or reassignment: nothing, read-only history, or her own notes only?Why we askClosed assignments hide the client today; the bank may want read-only access to the RM's own notes for a period.

04Asset classes supported

Per asset class: the products, the client-relevant lifecycle events, and the controls in front of a transaction. The workbench classifies holdings as cash and equivalents, fixed income, equity, alternatives, commodities and structured products, with credit facilities alongside.

Cash, deposits and FX

  1. Which cash products do clients hold: current accounts, term deposits, structured deposits, dual-currency investments, FX accumulators? Which does the bank classify as cash and which as structured products?Why we askClassification drives liquidity tiers and the coverage of cash needs; a structured deposit counted as cash overstates what is sellable.
  2. How is available liquidity defined: cleared, uncleared, pledged, under notice, reserved for settlement? Which may the RM quote as drawable?Why we askThe liquidity shortfall alert compares daily-liquid assets with twelve-month needs; the definition must match what the bank would actually release.
  3. Which FX transactions are client-initiated and which are funding conversions the bank makes? Are rate and spread visible per transaction?Why we askAttribution separates price, FX and flows; a bank conversion booked as a client trade misstates client behaviour in the vector.
  4. For deposits and accumulators: where are maturity, rollover instruction, fixings, knock-in and knock-out recorded, and when is the client told?Why we askMaturities and fixings are dated events the call plan should surface before they happen, not after.

Equities, ETFs and IPO

  1. Which service model applies per portfolio: discretionary, advisory, execution-only, custody? Who initiates orders in each and which fall under the RM's suitability duty?Why we askMandate bands are measured only on managed portfolios; the flag decides which breaches are the RM's problem.
  2. What single-position and issuer concentration limits apply at portfolio and household level, and do they look through ETFs, funds and notes to the issuer?Why we askLook-through concentration is the most visible alert in the workbench; the bank's limit definition must match or the alert is noise.
  3. How are IPO subscriptions and financing represented before listing?Why we askPre-listing exposure and the loan behind it otherwise vanish from the household view and from LTV.
  4. Which corporate actions reach the RM as decisions (rights, takeovers, scrip elections) versus processed silently, and by what deadline must the client elect?Why we askDated elections belong in the call plan's clock term; the RM should see them on the sheet before the deadline.
  5. Who computes realised and unrealised profit and loss, with which cost basis and currency, and does the client statement use the same figures?Why we askThe journey's performance story must agree with what the client holds in hand.

Funds and alternatives

  1. Which fund types are held: daily unit trusts, money-market funds, gated or locked funds, private equity and credit with capital calls, hedge funds with monthly or quarterly dealing?Why we askEach maps to a liquidity tier; the coverage ratio and the stress confidence band depend on it.
  2. For committed capital: where are commitment, called to date, uncalled and the expected call window, and how does a call notice arrive?Why we askUncalled commitments are dated cash needs the RM has to fund; a call notice is a Now item.
  3. How stale may a fund valuation be before it is flagged, and is the valuation date carried on the position?Why we askThe stale-valuation alert and the honesty of household totals depend on the date travelling with the number.

Bonds and structured products

  1. Are structured notes and equity-linked notes decomposed anywhere into underlyings, barriers, coupons and autocall dates, or held as a single line with a price?Why we askLook-through and the impact engine need the legs; today they come from a reference file the bank should replace with its structuring system.
  2. Which bond events reach the RM: coupon, call, maturity, rating change, restructuring? Where is the call schedule held?Why we askCalls and maturities are cash events; downgrades are signals against the household.
  3. Are pledged positions marked with lending value and advance rate per line?Why we askLine-level lending values let the impact engine recompute LTV under a scenario and explain a margin call.
  4. Which product risk rating scheme applies and how does it map to the client's profile? Where is the appropriateness assessment for complex products recorded?Why we askThe suitability gate on every idea and action must run the bank's rules, not ours.

Lending, collateral and mandates

  1. Which facilities exist against a household and is each linked to its collateral portfolio?Why we askThe facility-to-portfolio link is what makes LTV explainable line by line and lets the RM say which sale relieves it.
  2. How often is LTV recomputed, what are trigger, top-up and close-out levels, how much notice does the client get, and who may waive?Why we askHeadroom trend, the projected breach date and the waiver flag on the collateral board come from these.
  3. Where are mandate bands, target weights, single-position limits and exclusion lists held, and how are client-directed deviations recorded?Why we askMandate breaches feed the risk matrix; client-directed ones are a conduct item, not an RM failure, and must be distinguishable.
  4. What is the bank's risk-profile scale, horizon questionnaire and review cycle, and is comparing stated with observed behaviour acceptable to compliance?Why we askThe rubric scores capacity, appetite and horizon and flags stated-versus-observed mismatches; the bank must want that comparison.

Cross-asset controls

  1. Which cross-border rules apply by client residence and booking centre: what may be discussed, offered and sent, and what needs documented reverse enquiry?Why we askThe companion's guardrails and the communication gateway enforce a reference file; compliance owns the real matrix.
  2. How are complaints, vulnerable-client flags, deceased or dormant statuses represented, and must they stop outreach?Why we askEscalation triggers and the gateway need structured flags; free text in notes is not a control.

Where is the gap between the two, and why?Why we askThe difference between "final at 02:00" and "readable at 09:00" is usually a scheduling choice, not a system limit; it is the three hours we need.
  2. Can any source deliver incrementally (changes only) instead of a full file, and earlier than the consolidated batch?Why we askIncremental deliveries let the workbench apply overnight changes and be ready at six, then reconcile when the full batch lands.
  3. Which values can be restated after publication (late prices, corrected trades, NAV restatements) and how is a restatement signalled?Why we askA brief written at six from figures restated at nine must show that it was refreshed, and the audit trail must keep both.

08Event or micro-batch support

The workbench applies typed messages idempotently from Kafka or from files. Whether a source can emit events, deliver micro-batches, or only a daily file decides how early the brief can be ready and how current it stays through the day.

  1. Does each source publish business events today (message bus, webhooks, outbox), expose change data capture, deliver micro-batches at intervals, or only a daily file?Why we askDecides whether the Kafka front door or the batch adapter is the first integration per source, and what "current" can mean.
  2. For sources that emit events: is there a stable idempotency key and a business event time separate from publish time?Why we askThe landing zone dedupes on the key; the change log orders on event time; replay must be safe.
  3. Are events ordered per client or account, and can a consumer partition by client id?Why we askThe consumer partitions by client so a household's changes apply in order.
  4. How are late, out-of-order and corrected events represented?Why we askRestatements must be applied, not appended; the brief must say it was refreshed.
  5. Can topics be replayed from an offset or date, how long is retention, and is there a bootstrap path (snapshot topic or initial file)?Why we askA new consumer, a new RM book or a rebuilt vector store needs a cold start.
  6. For micro-batch sources: at what intervals, and can the interval be shortened overnight so the last batch lands before 05:30?Why we askMicro-batches every hour overnight would let the six o'clock run see nearly everything without any real-time integration.
  7. Which events carry personal data, and is tokenisation applied on the bus?Why we askDetermines what may sit in the landing zone and what must be masked before a model sees it.
  8. Who owns topic naming, schema registry and versioning, and who triages dead letters: producer or consumer team?Why we askMessage contracts are versioned; rejected messages appear in a dead-letter view that someone must own.
  9. What volumes and peaks per source (end of day, month end, corporate-action seasons)?Why we askSizes consumer batches and decides between book-wide and per-client rebuilds of vectors and alerts.
  10. Is there a production-like test environment with masked replay or synthetic feeds?Why we askIngestion must be proven against real shapes before the first six o'clock run.

09Agent build: content, ideas and communication

Imagine the RM at six. The brief is written, the calls are ranked, each client's story is ready. What else would she want the machine to have done overnight? Written the house view into words for each client. Turned this week's idea into the six clients it fits, each with a draft in their language. Prepared the messages she will send at nine, through the channel each client prefers, already passed through compliance. These questions establish whether the bank has the platforms and permissions for that, and what the RM would actually do with it.

Content authoring and publishing

  1. Does the bank have a content authoring and publishing platform for research and Chief Investment Office insights? Who writes, who approves, in which formats and languages, and is there a feed the workbench can read?Why we askHouse view is the source of signals and ideas; if it exists as documents and email only, the workbench must extract it, and approval status must travel with it.
  2. Is there a demand for personalised content per client: the house view rewritten for their holdings, their language and their profile, with the parts that do not apply removed?Why we askThe journey already writes prose from a client's facts; extending it to CIO content is a small step if compliance accepts model-rewritten text under approval.
  3. Which content may an RM adapt and which must be sent verbatim? Who signs off a personalised version, and how is the derivation from the approved original evidenced?Why we askThe gateway appends disclosures and logs edits today; personalised content needs a lineage from the approved source.
  4. Are trade ideas produced centrally (advisory desk, product specialists) or by RMs? How are they distributed, how is client fit assessed, and how is uptake measured?Why we askThe idea desk inverts ideas to clients with a suitability gate and tracks drafted and sent per idea; central ideas should enter it as data, not as a PDF.
  5. What does the RM do today with a new idea: read it, pick clients from memory, write the message by hand? How long does that take per idea?Why we askThat is the manual step the idea desk removes; the time saved is the business case.

Communicating with clients

  1. Through which channels may the bank contact clients: email, secure portal, mobile app notification, WhatsApp or other messaging, SMS, phone, letter? Which are approved for advice, which for information only, and per which jurisdiction?Why we askThe gateway checks every message; the channel decides which checks and disclosures apply and whether an agent may ever send.
  2. Where is client channel preference and consent recorded, including opt-outs and quiet hours?Why we askDrafts should default to the client's preferred channel and language; consent gates any agent-sent message.
  3. Is WhatsApp or another messaging platform in use with clients today, officially or unofficially, and is it archived and surveilled?Why we askIf it is the real channel, the workbench should draft for it and log it; if it is unofficial, that is a conduct finding for the team page, not a feature.
  4. Does the mobile app support personalised content or notifications per client, and can a message drafted by the RM be published into the client's app inbox?Why we askAn app inbox is the cleanest channel for agent-sent informational content, with read receipts and no forwarding risk.
  5. Which templates and disclosures must a client message carry by product, residence and channel, and who approves templates?Why we askThe gateway appends required disclosures; the bank's template library should replace the reference file.
  6. Is there an appetite for an agent to send low-risk informational messages itself (KYC reminder, meeting scheduling, market update, "since we last spoke" summary) with the RM notified, and what evidence would compliance want first?Why we askShadow mode measures agreement between what an agent would say and what the RM said; the bank sets the threshold that earns that step.
  7. What must be logged when a message is sent: content, channel, time, approver, disclosures, evidence used? Where must it land: the workbench, the CRM, the archive?Why we askEvery send is an audit event today; the bank may need it mirrored into its own archive for surveillance.

What the RM would do with the agents

  1. If the RM opened the workbench at six and found the brief, the call sheet, six personalised drafts and three proposed tasks, what would she change before acting, and what would she want to be asked first?Why we askDefines the confirm points: which outputs are ready to use, which need a glance, which need a decision.
  2. Which questions does the RM ask her assistant or her systems most often, in her words?Why we askThe assistant's grammar is a list of RM questions; the bank's list makes it useful on day one without a model.
  3. During a client call, what does the RM wish she could look up in seconds but cannot today?Why we askThe companion answers cues in a second from the engines; the wish list tells us which engines matter and which are missing.
  4. Which promises does the RM make most often, and how does she track them today?Why we askThe promise ledger extracts commitments from notes and drafts their fulfilment; the common ones deserve templates.
  5. What would make the RM trust a machine-written client story enough to read it aloud on a call?Why we askEvery sentence links to its rows; if trust needs something else (a human check, a house-approved phrase), the design should know.
  6. What would the team head want to see before allowing an agent to talk to a client at all, and what single switch would she want in her hand?Why we askThe machine panel shows agreement per playbook and a pause-all switch; the head's answer sets the threshold and the evidence.

10Calls to action and tasks

Information tells the RM what is true; insight tells her what it means; a call to action tells her what to do; a task is that action with an owner, a due date and evidence of completion. The workbench turns every insight into a proposed task the RM confirms, and every confirmation into an audit event. These questions establish what the bank's RMs actually do with what they learn, where tasks live today, and which ones a machine may prepare, propose or complete.

Questions about actions and tasks

  1. For each insight the workbench produces, what does an RM do about it today, and what would the bank want her to do? Are there insights that should produce no action at all?Why we askThe ranked actions and the call plan encode a mapping from insight to action; the bank's own expectation replaces our defaults, and some alerts may be for information only.
  2. Where do RM tasks live today: a CRM task list, a case-management tool, email, a spreadsheet, the RM's head? Who assigns them, and who else can see them?Why we askThe workbench proposes tasks and logs their completion; if the bank has a task system, tasks must be written there too, or the RM will keep two lists.
  3. Which tasks come to the RM from elsewhere: compliance (KYC, documentation), operations (failed settlement, missing form), the team head, the client, product desks? In what form and with what deadline?Why we askThe six o'clock task list should include what others need from the RM, not only what the data suggests; these need a feed or an inbox.
  4. How are tasks prioritised today when there are more than the day allows, and what happens to the ones that slip?Why we askThe call plan packs by deadline and priority and shows overflow explicitly; the bank's rules for what may slip decide the packing order.
  5. What counts as a task being done, and what evidence must exist: a note, a system update, a sent message, an approval?Why we askThe workbench marks tasks done from the event that proves it (send, decision, log) rather than a tick; each task type needs a defined proof.
  6. Which tasks have a regulatory or service-level deadline (KYC, margin call notice, complaint response, corporate-action election), and where are those deadlines held?Why we askDated deadlines drive the clock term of the call plan and the "returned early" rule on deferred clients.
  7. Which tasks may a machine complete outright (assemble documents, pre-fill a form, schedule from a calendar, draft a message), which may it propose for one-click confirmation, and which must the RM originate?Why we askThis is the autonomy line per task type: complete, propose, or prepare only. The workbench proposes today and confirms per card.
  8. May a task be assigned to someone other than the RM (assistant, operations, specialist), and may the machine route it there directly?Why we askSome calls to action are handoffs; routing needs the team model and the recipient's task system.
  9. How does the team head see task load and slippage across the team today, and what would she want escalated to her automatically?Why we askThe team page shows calls today, deferrals and approvals waiting; escalation rules decide what reaches her without asking.
  10. When an RM defers or dismisses a proposed task, must the reason be recorded, and does anyone review the pattern?Why we askDeferrals with reasons are logged and feed both the call plan and the conduct panel; the bank decides whether the pattern is reviewed.
  11. Which task outcomes should feed back into client data: a client's stated intention, a waiver, a preference, a promise kept?Why we askClosing a task often creates a fact (client chose to hold, prefers WhatsApp, will fund by Friday); those facts should land in structured fields, not only in a note.
  12. What would the RM want the six o'clock task list to look like: how many items, in what order, with what attached, and what should never be on it?Why we askDefines the shape of Today: the brief, the call sheet and the tasks are one page, and it must be readable in ninety seconds.

11The fifteen to ask first

If the first workshop has ninety minutes, these decide whether the six o'clock brief is possible and what it may contain.

  1. For custody positions and prices: at what time is yesterday final, at what time can we read it, and why the gap? (R-1)
  2. Which sources can deliver incrementally or in overnight micro-batches rather than one 9 a.m. file? (R-2, V-1, V-6)
  3. Which system is the golden source for the client and household, and for RM-to-client assignment with history? (S-1, E-1)
  4. Which identity provider issues RM identities, and which claims carry role and team? (E-4)
  5. Who else may see a client besides the primary RM, and is a team head's view the whole team or aggregates? (E-2, E-3)
  6. Which portfolios are discretionary, advisory, execution-only or custody, and where is that flag? (A-E1)
  7. Where are mandate bands, limits and exclusions codified, and do concentration limits look through? (A-L3, A-E2)
  8. How often is LTV recomputed, and where are triggers and waivers recorded? (A-L2)
  9. Are structured notes decomposed into legs, and are lending values carried per holding? (A-B1, A-B3)
  10. Where do notes, calls, client messages and dated cash needs live, in which languages? (Section 6, R-5)
  11. What is the bank's risk-profile scale, and may stated be compared with observed? (A-L4)
  12. Which suitability, appropriateness and cross-border rules must sit in front of every idea and message, and in what form can compliance supply them? (A-B4, A-X1)
  13. Is there a research and CIO content platform with a feed and approval status, and is personalised content wanted? (G-1, G-2)
  14. Which client channels are approved, where is consent recorded, and is an agent-sent informational message conceivable? (G-6, G-7, G-11)
  15. Where do RM tasks live today, what proves a task done, and which tasks may a machine complete rather than propose? (T-2, T-5, T-7)

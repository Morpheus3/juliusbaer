Agentic RM Workbench · discovery for Private Banking and Retail Wealth

Private Banking Discovery Questions

The bank's problem is a clock. Data lands at nine; the relationship manager needs her day ready at six: the brief, the calls, the client stories, the ideas and the tasks. These are the questions to ask so the workbench we built can be fed early enough, from the right systems, with the right entitlements, and so the agents that write content and reach clients do so within the bank's rules. Every question says why we ask it.

00How we are discovering

We start from what a relationship manager needs, not from systems. Six categories of need, taken from the workbench as built, are the spine of the whole page. Section 2 lists the information, insights and tasks under each category for the client to confirm. Sections 3 and 4 ask how the RM works and who may see what. Sections 5 and 6 ask what each need is made of. Sections 7 to 9 ask where it comes from and how fresh, complete and event-driven each source is, which is where the six o'clock answer lives. Sections 10 and 11 ask what the machine may write, send and do. Section 13 shows the data model that ties it together. Every question carries the category it serves, so the client can read the page by category or by section.

01The problem: 9 a.m. data, 6 a.m. need

Today the RM starts working when the data arrives. The goal is the reverse: when she opens the workbench at six, the machine has already read everything, ranked the book, written each client's story, run the signals against every household, found which clients an idea fits, drafted the messages, and lined up the tasks. The RM's morning is spent deciding, not assembling.

Three hours have to come from somewhere. Either the sources can deliver earlier or incrementally, or the workbench works from the previous close plus overnight events and refreshes when the day's batch lands. Which of those is possible is a question about each source system, and most of section 7 exists to answer it.

Today09:00 batch landsPositions, prices and balances arrive together; the RM reads, reconciles and plans until mid-morning.
Target04:00 to 05:30 machine runsOvernight events applied, vectors and alerts rebuilt, briefs and drafts written, call sheet packed, tasks proposed.
Target06:00 RM opens TodayBrief, call sheet, client journeys, ideas and drafts are ready. Anything that changes after the day's batch is marked as refreshed.
RuleNothing invented to fill the gapWhere a source cannot deliver by six, the screen shows the last known value with its timestamp, never a guess.

02What we built: information, insights and tasks to confirm

Everything the workbench produces today, grouped under the six categories of RM need and, within each, the sub-areas an RM thinks in. This is the list to put in front of the client first. For each row they mark keep, change, not needed or missing; the remaining sections then connect to the rows that survive, because every data, source, recency and event question below exists to feed one of these.

Information is a fact about the client or the market, read from the systems. Insight is a conclusion the machine draws and can explain, with a level 2 "why" and level 3 rows behind it. A task is a call to action with an owner, a due date and a defined proof of completion, proposed by the machine and confirmed by the RM.

03What the RM does

The workbench is organised around six moments of the RM's day: wake, prepare, converse, follow through, grow, reflect, with the team head's Monday above them. These questions confirm the bank's RM day matches, and what she does that we have not seen.

  1. Daily Planning & ConversationsWalk us through an RM's day from arrival to leaving: what she reads first, how she decides whom to call, what she prepares before a call, what she does during and after it, and what she hands to others.Why we askThe six moments we built are a hypothesis; the bank's real sequence decides what must be ready at six and in what order.
  2. Daily Planning & ConversationsHow many clients does an RM cover, how many conversations a day is normal, and what share of the day goes to preparation, documentation and chasing versus talking to clients?Why we askThe call plan's capacity and the efficiency case both rest on these numbers; the workbench should remove the preparation and documentation share.
  3. Client Intelligence & 360What does the RM assemble by hand today before a client call, and from how many screens or systems?Why we askThat assembly is the client journey's five chapters; whatever she gathers manually is what the machine must pre-build.
  4. Daily Planning & ConversationsHow does the RM decide whom to call first today, and whom she can leave until next week? Is it gut, a report, or a manager's list?Why we askThe call plan ranks by harm, deadline, bundling and relationship debt; the bank's actual heuristics should become its policy file, not be replaced by ours.
  5. Workflow, Outreach & GovernanceWhat must the RM record after every interaction (call note, suitability confirmation, disclosures given, promises made), where, and how long does it take?Why we askThe companion drafts the note, extracts promises and pre-fills the suitability record; the bank's evidence requirements define what "complete" means.
  6. Workflow, Outreach & GovernanceWhich decisions need a second person (checker, team head, compliance), and how long do those approvals take today?Why we askMaker-checker is configurable in the workflow; approval latency is a task the machine can queue and chase before six.
  7. Market Signals & RiskWhat does the RM do when a market event happens intraday: who tells her, how does she find which clients are affected, and what is she expected to do by when?Why we askSignals mapped to holdings and the impact engine automate exactly this; the expected response time decides whether intraday events must flow, not just the overnight batch.
  8. Team & Data PlatformWhat does the team head look at on Monday morning, and what does she ask RMs for by email today?Why we askThe team page has five panels built on a guess of those questions; the real ones replace them.
  9. Team & Data PlatformWhich parts of the day would the RM most like to stop doing, and which would she never hand to a machine?Why we askSets the autonomy ladder honestly: drafts only, approve to send, or informational messages sent by an agent.

04Entitlements for the RM

One RM must not see another's clients. The workbench enforces this in the database with row-level security driven by dated RM-to-client assignments. These questions establish where that truth lives in the bank.

  1. Team & Data PlatformWhere is the RM-to-client assignment held (CRM, core banking, HR), and is history kept with valid-from and valid-to dates?Why we askEntitlement is computed from assignments valid today; the audit trail needs history to answer who could see a client in a given month.
  2. Team & Data PlatformBesides the primary RM, who else legitimately sees a client: secondary RM, assistant, checker, team head, holiday cover, product specialist, compliance? Is each a role with a duration?Why we askOur assignment model has primary, secondary, checker and cover roles with dates; the bank's list decides which roles exist and whether cover is time-boxed.
  3. Team & Data PlatformHow is a team or desk defined, who heads it, and may a head see every client in the team or only aggregates?Why we askTeam scope in row-level security is the head's whole team; if the bank wants aggregates only, the team page must change.
  4. Team & Data PlatformWhich identity provider issues RM and manager identities, and which claims carry role, team and RM id?Why we askThe API verifies a token and maps its claims to scope; production needs the bank's issuer, audience and claim names.
  5. Team & Data PlatformWhen a client is reassigned, how quickly must the old RM lose access and the new RM gain it, and who approves the reassignment?Why we askAn assignment message updates access the moment it is applied; the bank's expectation decides whether that must be an event or can wait for a nightly file.
  6. Team & Data PlatformMay any book-wide computation happen (percentiles, peer groups, which clients fit an idea) as long as an RM only ever sees her own clients in the result?Why we askThe analytics service computes peers and percentiles across the bank under a service role; the idea desk searches the whole book then filters to the caller's scope.
  7. Team & Data PlatformWhat may an RM see about a client after offboarding or reassignment: nothing, read-only history, or her own notes only?Why we askClosed assignments hide the client today; the bank may want read-only access to the RM's own notes for a period.

05Asset classes supported

Per asset class: the products, the client-relevant lifecycle events, and the controls in front of a transaction. The workbench classifies holdings as cash and equivalents, fixed income, equity, alternatives, commodities and structured products, with credit facilities alongside.

Cash, deposits and FX

  1. Client Intelligence & 360Which cash products do clients hold: current accounts, term deposits, structured deposits, dual-currency investments, FX accumulators? Which does the bank classify as cash and which as structured products?Why we askClassification drives liquidity tiers and the coverage of cash needs; a structured deposit counted as cash overstates what is sellable.
  2. Client Intelligence & 360How is available liquidity defined: cleared, uncleared, pledged, under notice, reserved for settlement? Which may the RM quote as drawable?Why we askThe liquidity shortfall alert compares daily-liquid assets with twelve-month needs; the definition must match what the bank would actually release.
  3. Client Intelligence & 360Which FX transactions are client-initiated and which are funding conversions the bank makes? Are rate and spread visible per transaction?Why we askAttribution separates price, FX and flows; a bank conversion booked as a client trade misstates client behaviour in the vector.
  4. Client Intelligence & 360For deposits and accumulators: where are maturity, rollover instruction, fixings, knock-in and knock-out recorded, and when is the client told?Why we askMaturities and fixings are dated events the call plan should surface before they happen, not after.

Equities, ETFs and IPO

  1. Client Intelligence & 360Which service model applies per portfolio: discretionary, advisory, execution-only, custody? Who initiates orders in each and which fall under the RM's suitability duty?Why we askMandate bands are measured only on managed portfolios; the flag decides which breaches are the RM's problem.
  2. Market Signals & RiskWhat single-position and issuer concentration limits apply at portfolio and household level, and do they look through ETFs, funds and notes to the issuer?Why we askLook-through concentration is the most visible alert in the workbench; the bank's limit definition must match or the alert is noise.
  3. Client Intelligence & 360How are IPO subscriptions and financing represented before listing?Why we askPre-listing exposure and the loan behind it otherwise vanish from the household view and from LTV.
  4. Daily Planning & ConversationsWhich corporate actions reach the RM as decisions (rights, takeovers, scrip elections) versus processed silently, and by what deadline must the client elect?Why we askDated elections belong in the call plan's clock term; the RM should see them on the sheet before the deadline.
  5. Client Intelligence & 360Who computes realised and unrealised profit and loss, with which cost basis and currency, and does the client statement use the same figures?Why we askThe journey's performance story must agree with what the client holds in hand.

Funds and alternatives

  1. Market Signals & RiskWhich fund types are held: daily unit trusts, money-market funds, gated or locked funds, private equity and credit with capital calls, hedge funds with monthly or quarterly dealing?Why we askEach maps to a liquidity tier; the coverage ratio and the stress confidence band depend on it.
  2. Market Signals & RiskFor committed capital: where are commitment, called to date, uncalled and the expected call window, and how does a call notice arrive?Why we askUncalled commitments are dated cash needs the RM has to fund; a call notice is a Now item.
  3. Client Intelligence & 360How stale may a fund valuation be before it is flagged, and is the valuation date carried on the position?Why we askThe stale-valuation alert and the honesty of household totals depend on the date travelling with the number.

Bonds and structured products

  1. Market Signals & RiskAre structured notes and equity-linked notes decomposed anywhere into underlyings, barriers, coupons and autocall dates, or held as a single line with a price?Why we askLook-through and the impact engine need the legs; today they come from a reference file the bank should replace with its structuring system.
  2. Market Signals & RiskWhich bond events reach the RM: coupon, call, maturity, rating change, restructuring? Where is the call schedule held?Why we askCalls and maturities are cash events; downgrades are signals against the household.
  3. Market Signals & RiskAre pledged positions marked with lending value and advance rate per line?Why we askLine-level lending values let the impact engine recompute LTV under a scenario and explain a margin call.
  4. Ranked Actions & IdeasWhich product risk rating scheme applies and how does it map to the client's profile? Where is the appropriateness assessment for complex products recorded?Why we askThe suitability gate on every idea and action must run the bank's rules, not ours.

Lending, collateral and mandates

  1. Market Signals & RiskWhich facilities exist against a household and is each linked to its collateral portfolio?Why we askThe facility-to-portfolio link is what makes LTV explainable line by line and lets the RM say which sale relieves it.
  2. Market Signals & RiskHow often is LTV recomputed, what are trigger, top-up and close-out levels, how much notice does the client get, and who may waive?Why we askHeadroom trend, the projected breach date and the waiver flag on the collateral board come from these.
  3. Market Signals & RiskWhere are mandate bands, target weights, single-position limits and exclusion lists held, and how are client-directed deviations recorded?Why we askMandate breaches feed the risk matrix; client-directed ones are a conduct item, not an RM failure, and must be distinguishable.
  4. Market Signals & RiskWhat is the bank's risk-profile scale, horizon questionnaire and review cycle, and is comparing stated with observed behaviour acceptable to compliance?Why we askThe rubric scores capacity, appetite and horizon and flags stated-versus-observed mismatches; the bank must want that comparison.

Cross-asset controls

  1. Workflow, Outreach & GovernanceWhich cross-border rules apply by client residence and booking centre: what may be discussed, offered and sent, and what needs documented reverse enquiry?Why we askThe companion's guardrails and the communication gateway enforce a reference file; compliance owns the real matrix.
  2. Workflow, Outreach & GovernanceHow are complaints, vulnerable-client flags, deceased or dormant statuses represented, and must they stop outreach?Why we askEscalation triggers and the gateway need structured flags; free text in notes is not a control.

  3. Team & Data PlatformWhich values can be restated after publication (late prices, corrected trades, NAV restatements) and how is a restatement signalled?Why we askA brief written at six from figures restated at nine must show that it was refreshed, and the audit trail must keep both.
  4. Team & Data PlatformWhat data-quality checks already run per source, where are failures visible, and who fixes them?Why we askThe workbench's thirteen checks and dead-letter view should extend the bank's controls, and someone must own the failures.
  5. Team & Data PlatformWhat share of clients, portfolios and instruments does each source cover? Which clients or products are outside it (external custody, held-away assets, insurance)?Why we askA household total that omits held-away assets misleads the RM; gaps should be shown as gaps.
  6. Team & Data PlatformHow much history is online per source and are daily point-in-time snapshots kept?Why we askMomentum, stress windows, behavioural features and the replay clock need dated history, not just current state.
  7. Team & Data PlatformIs the client's reporting-currency conversion done at source or expected downstream, with which rate and time?Why we askHousehold totals and FX effects are only comparable under one convention.

Household is the one entity we propose adding: today the client is the household.
